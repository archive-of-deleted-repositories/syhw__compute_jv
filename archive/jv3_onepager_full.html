What this is

A three-member neolab cooperative — not a merchant data center

Three AI labs form a joint venture to build the compute they each need, at cost, on one French fast-track site. Each member takes an equal ~7MW slice of every tranche and signs a take-or-pay contract guaranteeing it — so demand is 100% committed from day one, with no dependence on an external anchor tenant. The build phases from today's GB300 into Vera Rubin as it ships.

Equal thirds
Each member funds and controls its own ~7MW sleeve in every tranche; the campus is shared and cost-split 1/3.
Self-guaranteed demand
Each member's take-or-pay is the bankable contract — the members are both the owners and the customers.
Phased, France
Dunkerque or Le Bosquel fast-track site; 800VDC-ready shell so later Rubin tranches drop into reserved corridors.

The seven tranches — in MW and $

Two GB300 tranches now, three Rubin tranches next — two Rubin-Ultra options beyond

Every tranche is 20MW of IT load (~24MW gross at PUE 1.20 — grid and utilities are sized at 24MW). T1–T2 are committed; T3–T5 are the committed path, re-priced at NVIDIA's spec freeze; T6–T7 are options with re-underwriting gates, not fixed commitments — the Rubin Ultra design was still moving as of mid-2026.

Who does what

The JV owns the powered infrastructure — the neolabs own the compute

A clean division of labor and risk: the JV is the shared platform (land, power, shell, allocation, operations); each neolab funds and runs its own GPU sleeve and guarantees its own demand. Real-estate risk sits with the JV; GPU-obsolescence risk sits with the members, hedged by their take-or-pay.

The JV · shared platform (PropCo + OpCo)

  • Site control, permits, RTE 400kV grid connection + power (PPA)
  • Powered shell — building, electrical, liquid-cooling plant, 800VDC backbone
  • Holds the master NVIDIA allocation; distributes to members
  • Shared network fabric, security, 24/7 campus operations & facility SLAs
  • Finances & owns PropCo (infra debt + equity + sovereign/EU capital); runs EPC delivery
  • Bears shell / real-estate residual risk (15–30yr life)

Each neolab · its own ChipCo sleeve (~7MW)

  • Funds its ~1/3 sleeve equity and its GPU purchase (deposit + equity + sleeve debt)
  • Signs & self-guarantees its take-or-pay — the bankable demand contract
  • Provides VC-backer credit support (LC / guarantee) behind its sleeve debt
  • Runs its own workloads, software / MLOps, and refresh decisions
  • Bears GPU obsolescence risk (4–6yr) on its sleeve
  • Pays capacity fees / prepayment into the JV (funds the debt-service reserve)

Base financing

Blended stack, sequenced by tranche — self-funding as it scales

The members' take-or-pay is the single most valuable financing input — but three venture-backed labs are a sub-investment-grade credit, so the base case is underwritten on hard member support alone. Debt sizes on the reserved-capacity leg of the contract (payable irrespective of usage), not on utilization. Public capital and NVIDIA support are upside levers that improve the base — they do not define it.

First-close debt: DSCR ≥1.4× on the reserved leg, advance ≤40–45% of depreciable GPU cost, ~8.5–10.5% — re-pricing toward CoreWeave's IG benchmark (~SOFR+2.25%) only after seasoning. Prepayment (6–12mo fees) funds the DSRA. Upside levers: an EU InvestAI/Bpifrance award (−10–17pp equity, underwritten only on a term sheet) and an NVIDIA backstop (+debt, −150–300bps — priced strategic capital, not free).

The recycling flywheel — PropCo-first

The base takeout is the shell, not the chips: once a block runs 18–24 months under its take-or-pay, the PropCo refinances into a euro data-center ABS (Vantage's €640M EMEA deal is the template), returning equity for the next tranche. ChipCo refinancing — where GPU residuals can compress in one product cycle — is upside only. Members top up equity checks with low-coupon convertibles (Nebius's 1–3% notes).

Per member: ~$1.60B firm (T1–T5), ~$3.0B with options — funded on a revolving basis. First-tranche upfront cash per member ≈ $60–140M (development share + GPU deposit + first equity draws at the 50% base).

Alternative funding strategies

Seven options — blend them, sequenced by tranche

No single source funds a ~$9B program. Underwrite the base on member credit alone; layer the rest as levers — each anchored to a real 2024–2026 precedent.

Option 1 · base
Member equity + credit-enhanced GPU-backed debt
Delayed-draw term loan in an SPV, sized on the reserved-capacity leg of the members' take-or-pay. First close prices wide (~SOFR+450–650); the IG benchmark is the refinancing target after seasoning, not day one.
Precedent: CoreWeave — 14–15% debt in 2023 → DDTL 4.0 at SOFR+2.25% / A3/A− by 2026, only after IG-customer backlog seasoned.
Option 2 · lever
Sovereign / EU public capital
EU covers up to ~17% of CAPEX + Bpifrance equity + "Choose France" halo. Cheapest, non-dilutive — pursue hard, but underwrite only on a term sheet or award, never in the base case.
Precedent: Campus AI (MGX / Bpifrance / Mistral / NVIDIA) €8.5B; EU InvestAI €20B for AI gigafactories; Bpifrance €10B AI envelope.
Option 3
Sale-leaseback / OpCo–PropCo with an infra fund
Sell the shell (and/or GPUs) to an infra fund; lease back long-term. Recycles equity; fund absorbs the hard-asset risk.
Precedent: Meta–Blue Owl "Hyperion" ~$30B (80/20); Crusoe–Blue Owl Abilene ~$15B, 15yr Oracle lease.
Option 4 · lever
NVIDIA + OEM vendor financing
An NVIDIA capacity backstop tightens debt spreads (−150–300bps); OEM deferred/lease terms cut upfront cash. Powerful — but priced strategic capital: NVIDIA takes concentration and option value, so model it as a lever, not free money.
Precedent: NVIDIA–CoreWeave $6.3B backstop (to 2032); Lambda–Macquarie $500M GPU facility.
Option 5
Infra co-equity partner (GP / promote)
An infra fund funds the equity bulk (~80/20); the three members keep GP control + carried upside above a hurdle.
Precedent: Meta–Blue Owl 80/20; DigitalBridge Partners III raised $11.7B for exactly this.
Option 6 · take-out
Euro data-center ABS — PropCo only
At 18–24 months of stabilized take-or-pay history, refinance the shell into ABS to recycle equity. The takeout market buys long-life infrastructure with strong contracts — not short-life chips; ChipCo takeout is upside only.
Precedent: Vantage EMEA €640M (first euro DC ABS, Class A A−); Switch $659M ABS.
Option 7 · member level
Convertibles / venture debt
Members fund their equity checks with low-coupon convertibles at HoldCo — cheap, fast, no immediate dilution.
Precedent: Nebius $1B converts (2.00% '29 / 3.00% '31), then ~$4.2B in 2025.
Option 8 · optional upside
Forward capacity sale / prepay
Graft an external anchor (Meta/Nebius-style) onto a tranche with a 15–25% prepay to buy back leverage and seed the ABS — not required, pure upside.
Precedent: Microsoft–CoreWeave ~$10B, customers prepay 15–25%.
Recommended sequence: underwrite T1–T2 on member equity + hard backer credit support + first-close DDTL (1) — this must stand on its own → split OpCo/PropCo from day one → pursue sovereign/EU capital (2) and NVIDIA support (4) in parallel as levers that walk equity down and spreads in → recycle via PropCo ABS (6) at month 18–24 → bring an infra co-equity partner (5) if scaling beyond member capacity → exercise the T6–T7 Rubin-Ultra options only at spec freeze, re-underwritten. Keep the GP/promote and long-run upside with the three neolabs.

Getting started

First moves

  1. Form TopCo + sleeve template. Three-member agreement: equal thirds, 48-rack GB300 allocation, per-sleeve autonomy, 2-of-3 supermajority + buy-sell.
  2. Paper the self-guarantee. Each member signs a two-leg take-or-pay (reserved-capacity + usage) and secures VC-backer credit support — JV3's gating item. Nail the governance hard points (cure periods, sleeve isolation, buy-sell, insolvency step-in) — lenders read these before pricing.
  3. Anchor the public capital. Bid into EU InvestAI / EuroHPC gigafactory call; open Bpifrance / Caisse des Dépôts; "Choose France."
  4. Lock allocation + site. 144-rack GB300 allocation + NVIDIA backstop; exclusivity at Dunkerque / Le Bosquel; RTE 20MW-tranche phasing.
  5. Mandate the bank. Structuring + warehouse to fund GPU deposits; ABS-take-out and co-equity conversations in parallel.
  6. FID Tranche 1 (GB300). Each member funds ~$50–120M; first drawdown; 800VDC-ready shell so Rubin tranches drop in.
